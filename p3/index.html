Key Takeaways

  • New dataset: 1,810 U.S. infrastructure projects → 791 P3s → 389 closed, privately financed P3s ($254B total capex), linking funding stacks, revenue contracts, sponsors, and post-deal outcomes.
  • Risk & leverage at close: Relative to operating-only deals, financial-led P3s are +23 pp more likely to use government-paid contracts and carry +11 pp higher leverage (+19 pp under government-pay).
  • Horizon & outcomes: Financial-led stakes are sold 2.5× faster (3.5× for closed-end funds vs. long-horizon investors), with no differences in delay, renegotiation, or early termination.
Read full abstract

Public-private partnerships (P3s) are the primary channel through which private capital invests in public infrastructure, yet little is known about the financing, risk-taking, or long-term ownership of these projects. I assemble the first project-level dataset of 791 U.S. P3s, linking funding sources, contract terms, sponsors, and subsequent outcomes. Compared with projects led by construction firms, projects led by financial investors have higher leverage, higher government-retained demand risk, and higher government-guaranteed payments. Financial-led projects are sold to other investors sooner despite no differences in delay or renegotiation. Overall, financial investors' actions are not aligned with the primary economic arguments behind P3s.
